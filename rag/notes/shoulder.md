## Limit or avoid
High strain at the extreme ends of the shoulder's range of motion. Heavy or fast pressing, wide grips and deep pressing, where the elbows travel far from the body and toward the floor, all add stress to the shoulder (the AC joint). Pressing too often, or always with the same grip, angle and range, can lead to overuse.

## Why the shoulder needs care
Unlike the hip and knee, the shoulder isn't built to bear weight, so it is prone to overuse. Stress itself isn't bad: the aim is to adjust it so the shoulder adapts and gets stronger while the injury is respected.

## Modifying the bench press
Lower the load or slow the lift: use more reps with a lighter weight, or add a pause at the bottom. Press less often and with fewer sets in the week, swapping one or two pressing exercises for rows. Change the press: incline or decline, a different grip width, a neutral-grip bar, or dumbbells. Narrow the grip and shorten the range with a close-grip bench, floor press, board press or Spoto press. If pressing still hurts, replace it with a row such as a seal row, chest-supported row or single-arm dumbbell row.

## Balance pressing with pulling
For every chest or pressing exercise, do two back or shoulder-blade exercises, such as bent-over shoulder extensions, reverse flys and bent-over rows. Bent-over row: lean on a bed or counter, raise the arm while bending the elbow and squeeze the shoulder blade toward the spine, 3 sets of 10 with a light 3 to 5 lb weight or whatever feels easy.

## Strengthen the rotator cuff
With a towel roll under the upper arm and the elbow kept at the side, use an elastic band. Internal rotation: pull the band in toward the stomach, 3 sets of 10 to 15. External rotation: start with the hand near the stomach and pull the band outward, 3 sets of 10.

## Progressing safely
Keep the hands within peripheral vision during pressing and overhead lifts. Add one new exercise at a time, so any soreness can be traced to it. Increase reps and sets before adding weight. Keep a neutral spine and avoid slouching.

## Lower-body training
Team guidance, not from the sources above: while the shoulder recovers, put more of the training focus on the legs, as the ankle and knee notes do for the upper body. Prefer machine leg work where the arms don't hold or support the weight, such as leg press, leg extension and leg curl, since a barbell on the back or heavy dumbbells in the hands still load the shoulder.

## When to stop and get checked
Stop if there is discomfort or pain and rest for a day or so. If the discomfort persists, get a professional assessment to rule out further injury.
