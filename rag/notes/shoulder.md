## Keep it moving
Sources: https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/shoulder-pain/
Staying active and gently moving the shoulder helps, while avoiding things that seem to make it worse. Stopping using the shoulder completely isn't recommended, and neither is slouching when sitting. Making up strenuous exercises or using heavy gym equipment is best avoided with shoulder pain. Recovery can take 6 months or longer.

## Strengthen the rotator cuff
Sources: https://www.orthoinfo.org/en/recovery/rotator-cuff-and-shoulder-conditioning-program/
Resistance band rotations are done with the elbow pressed into the side: internal rotation brings the arm across the body, external rotation rotates it outward. They usually start at 3 sets of 8. A standing row, squeezing the shoulder blades together, works the upper back.

## Progressing safely
Sources: https://www.orthoinfo.org/en/recovery/rotator-cuff-and-shoulder-conditioning-program/
As an exercise gets easier, it can build up from 3 sets of 8 to 3 sets of 12 repetitions. An exercise shouldn't cause pain.

## Pressing with a sore shoulder
Sources: team guidance
These options are for after the shoulder settles or a physiotherapist clears it, since heavy gym work is best avoided while it's painful. Heavier or faster pressing adds shoulder stress, and wide grips and deep pressing especially stress the AC joint. High strain at the extreme ends of the range, or always pressing with the same grip, angle and range, can lead to overuse. Bench press options: a lighter weight for more reps, a pause at the bottom, fewer pressing sets in the week with one or two swapped for rows, incline or decline, a different grip width, a neutral-grip bar, dumbbells, or a shorter range such as a close-grip bench, floor press or board press. If pressing hurts, the usual step is to stop pressing and switch to a chest-supported or single-arm dumbbell row.

## Balance and lower-body work
Sources: team guidance
Two back or shoulder-blade exercises for every pressing exercise, such as reverse flys and bent-over rows with a light weight, keep things balanced. Keeping the hands within peripheral vision during pressing and overhead lifts, and adding one new exercise at a time, makes soreness easier to trace. Leg machines where the arms don't hold the weight, such as leg press, leg extension and leg curl, keep training going without loading the shoulder.

## When to get checked
Sources: https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/shoulder-pain/, https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/sprains-and-strains/
Get emergency care straight away after an injury if you heard a crack, the shoulder has changed shape or is pointing at an odd angle, the arm is numb, tingling or has pins and needles, or the skin has turned blue or grey or is cold to touch. Get urgent medical advice for sudden or very bad shoulder pain, if you can't move your arm, the arm or shoulder has changed shape or is badly swollen, pins and needles don't go away, there's no feeling in the arm or shoulder, it is hot or cold to touch, the pain started after an injury or accident like a fall, both shoulders are severely painful, or you feel feverish or unwell. See a doctor if shoulder pain is getting worse or does not improve after 2 weeks, or it's very difficult to move the arm or shoulder.
