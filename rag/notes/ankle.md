## Types of ankle injury
Ankle injuries can be acute, such as sprains, high ankle sprains, fractures and tendon tears, or chronic, such as Achilles tendonitis, arthritis and ongoing instability after an injury that didn't heal well. The type of injury decides the treatment and how long recovery takes.

## Sprain grades and recovery time
Grade 1 is a light sprain, usually healed in about 2 to 3 weeks. Grade 2 is a bigger ligament injury, usually 4 to 6 weeks. Grade 3 is a full ligament tear, sometimes with a fracture, and can take 3 months or more. Every case is different, and going back to a normal routine too early risks re-injury.

## First days: RICE
Rest from anything that hurts. Ice for 10 to 20 minutes several times a day with a cloth between ice and skin, usually for about the first three days. Compress with an elastic bandage that is snug but not tight, for no more than 72 hours. Elevate the ankle to heart level or higher to reduce swelling. A severe sprain may need a brace for up to 6 weeks.

## Limit or avoid
Running, jumping and walking long distances. Each heel strike while running puts roughly three to four times body weight through the foot, which an injured foot or ankle can't take. Keep load off the legs while the ankle heals.

## Strength swaps
Train the upper body lying or sitting on a bench, so nothing presses on the foot: bench press, incline bench press, bicep curls, tricep extensions or kickbacks, and side lateral raises. Core exercises done lying on your back also keep weight off the ankle.

## Cardio swaps
A stationary bike, even while wearing a walking boot or brace, though pedalling still puts some stress on the foot. Swimming or walking laps in a pool, where the water takes weight off the foot. Check with a healthcare provider before starting either.

## Rehab exercises
A doctor may have you put small amounts of weight on the ankle within the first week, or after 1 to 2 days for a mild sprain. Once weight bearing is comfortable, a physical therapy program helps restore balance, range of motion and strength, starting small and building up. Typical exercises include a standing calf stretch, ankle circles, ankle alphabet, band pushes, towel curls, heel raises, wall pushes and one-leg balance. Two simple ones: press the foot against a sturdy object while lifting the heel and hold about 10 seconds, a few times, three to four times a day; and sitting with the leg straight, trace the alphabet in the air with the big toe. A provider may ask for a brace or stability bandage during these to keep the ankle from moving too much.

## When to check with a professional
Before starting any exercise on an injured ankle, including cycling or swimming; to judge how severe the injury is and whether a sports injury specialist should be involved; and to set up a rehab program. Full return to normal training should wait until range of motion, strength, balance and swelling are all back to acceptable levels.
