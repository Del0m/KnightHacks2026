## Basics while the back is sore
Keep moving rather than resting completely: walking or other light aerobic work, and gentle mobility when it eases stiffness. Temporarily lower the load or shorten the range of motion, avoid repeating reps that make symptoms spike, and bring squats and hip hinges back gradually.

## Limit or avoid
Testing heavy single reps too early, pushing through sharp or worsening pain, making big jumps in the program after a few good days, and treating complete inactivity as the only safe option.

## Early phase: calm symptoms and keep moving
Walking, light core and hip work, bodyweight movement patterns, light goblet squats or supported variations if they feel fine, a reduced range of motion, and light sled work or other controlled conditioning.

## Mid phase: rebuild tolerance to loading
Light to moderate squatting, relearning the hip hinge, split squats and single-leg strength work, controlled pulling and carrying, and a gradual return to barbell lifts, as confidence in daily activities and gym movements grows.

## Late phase: rebuild strength
Progressively heavier barbell squats and deadlifts, harder compound lifts, more training volume, sport-specific strength work, and a return to explosive lifts if appropriate.

## How to tell if progress is too fast
Training should feel challenging without a major flare of symptoms during the session, later that day, or the next morning. Mild soreness can be fine. A clear flare that lingers or gets worse means the step up was too aggressive.

## Common mistakes
Returning just because pain was gone for one day, jumping straight back to pre-injury weights, ignoring sleep, stress and recovery, avoiding all spinal loading out of fear, loading too heavily because a good warm-up felt easy, and copying someone else's return plan instead of matching your own tolerance.

## Preventing flare-ups
Build load gradually, keep technique consistent especially when tired, protect sleep and recovery, and don't follow time off with a sudden maximal effort. Train the hips, trunk and single-leg control, warm up for the day's lifting, lower volume when life stress is high, and watch how symptoms respond over the next 24 hours.
